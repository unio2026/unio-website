Ideas / A different way of doing business

Can business work differently?

A business can sell useful products and services while choosing different rules for who benefits from its success.

Beyond shareholder-first distributions

Traditional private businesses may distribute profits to owners. Other models put greater emphasis on employees, customers, communities or reinvestment. These approaches differ in ownership, control and how financial benefits are shared.

Examples of alternative approaches

Cooperatives are typically organised around member participation. Employee-owned businesses give workers an ownership stake. Social enterprises pursue defined social purposes. Loyalty schemes reward customers, usually without granting a share of profit.

Where UNIO fits

UNIO proposes a privately owned commercial system with a non-extraction principle: ownership alone should not entitle its founder to withdraw profits for personal use. Its proposed distribution would divide future distributable profits equally between eligible customers and workers.

An idea still being built

UNIO's governance, services and eligibility rules remain under development. It is not claiming to be a cooperative, employee-owned company or currently operating profit-sharing scheme.
