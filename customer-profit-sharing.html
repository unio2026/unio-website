Ideas / A different way of doing business

Can a business share profits with its customers?

Customers help sustain businesses through their purchases. Could some of the resulting success return to them?

Why customer loyalty matters

A repeat customer contributes revenue, helps sustain demand and may recommend a business to others. Most conventional loyalty programmes reward purchases with points, discounts or perks rather than a share of profit.

Loyalty rewards are not profit sharing

A discount reduces the price of a purchase. Profit sharing would instead depend on the business having a defined amount of distributable profit and rules for allocating it. Revenue and profit are not interchangeable.

How UNIO proposes to approach it

UNIO is exploring a model in which half of future distributable profit would be allocated to eligible participating customers in proportion to qualifying spending. The other half would be allocated to eligible workers based on qualifying hours. This is a proposal, not an existing payment programme.

What would need to be established?

A real programme would need eligibility rules, accounting standards, reserves, legal governance, a distribution timetable and clear consumer protections. No returns or entitlements currently exist through UNIO.
